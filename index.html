What changed recently

Ten updates that reshape the menu
Enacted · Sept 29, 2026 · takes effect Jan 1, 2027
SB 1370 codifies the emergency fuels-streamlining process

SB 1370 (Stern; Ch. 777, Stats. 2026) adds PRC §§4240–4243: a single consolidated state review (CNRA with CalEPA, through a CAL FIRE-led project review team) for covered wildfire mitigation projects. CEQA does not apply to authorized projects (§4240(d)). It is a time-limited version of the durable successor to the March 2025 emergency process that the June 2026 draft Action Plan (Key Action 3.7) called for.

Covered work

Hazard-tree removal, strategic fuel breaks (≤300 ft wide), defensible space (≤200 ft from a structure), evacuation-route clearance (≤100 ft from centerline), forest resilience treatments, nonnative removal in shrublands and grasslands, fuel-break maintenance, utility vegetation management, and prescribed or cultural fire in fire-deficit ecosystems (not chaparral or coastal sage scrub). The project must be required by law or identified in a utility wildfire mitigation plan, community wildfire preparedness plan, local-agency plan, or CAL FIRE plan. Size caps: <1,000 ac forest, <3,000 ac utility right-of-way, <50 ac shrubland nonnative-grass removal.

Limits

Projects that use pesticides, or that would need a CESA or Native Plant Protection Act take permit, are ineligible. An expedited authorization can waive other state requirements within CNRA/CalEPA jurisdiction, but not Clean Water Act–delegated permits, water-rights requirements (Water Code Div. 2), the Forest Practice Act for timber operations, or local ordinances.

Process

20-day public comment on each application; decision within 60 days of a complete application; work done within 2 years (5 for prescribed fire). Compliance with the Statewide Fuels Reduction Environmental Protection Plan is mandatory (civil penalties up to $10,000 per violation).

Timing

Authority to issue expedited authorizations ends Jan 1, 2029; the chapter is repealed Jan 1, 2034. SB 1370 also directs the Board of Forestry to build a Nontimber Vegetation Management Program as a CEQA certified regulatory program (emergency regulations due by July 1, 2028). Agencies have not yet posted the application process, and the final Action Plan (expected fall 2026) had not been released as of Oct 1, 2026.

Enacted · Sept 2026
Good Fire Act adds a CAL FIRE burn-assistance exemption; FPA plan caps rise

CEQA no longer applies to CAL FIRE’s assistance on prescribed and cultural burns (PRC §21080.49.1). A separate bill raises NTMP and WFMP acreage caps.

Read moreShow less
AB 1699 (Rogers; Ch. 761, Stats. 2026), an urgency statute in effect since it was signed Sept 29, 2026, adds PRC §21080.49.1: CEQA does not apply to actions CAL FIRE takes to assist prescribed fire or cultural burning projects that do not otherwise require CEQA compliance (this codifies the EO N-35-25 suspension). It also makes the Prescribed Fire Liability Pilot Program permanent, moves burn-boss recertification to no sooner than every 3 years, and lets CAL FIRE waive burn-permit site inspections for specified burners. Its change to PRC §4799.05 affects only grant advance-payment reporting, so the federal-NEPA exemption’s Jan 1, 2028 sunset is unchanged. Separately, AB 442 (Hadwick; Ch. 504, Stats. 2026) raises NTMP caps to 4,000 ac and WFMP caps to 15,000 ac, and drops the single-hydrologic-area limit on WFMP harvest areas.

The four pathway types — color-coded throughout this reference
Streamlined pathway
A statutory or categorical exemption from CEQA. File a Notice of Exemption — no Initial Study, no EIR. Statutory exemptions (§4799.05(d), SB 131, SERP) are the strongest because the §15300.2 exceptions don’t apply.
Programmatic pathway
Tier from an existing Program EIR — most commonly the CalVTP PEIR (prepare a Project-Specific Analysis), or coordinate with the local CAL FIRE Unit on an active Vegetation Management Program (VMP) project. Or proceed under a Forest Practice document (THP / NTMP / PTEIR / FPR exemption) that serves as a CEQA functional equivalent. Some regions also have local or agency PEIRs (fire district or park district vegetation management plans) that can be tiered from within their jurisdiction.
Standard CEQA document
Full project-specific review: Initial Study → Negative Declaration, Mitigated Negative Declaration, or Environmental Impact Report. Lead agency files a Notice of Determination.
Expired / closed pathway
No longer available for new applications. Shown for context — e.g. the March 2025 Emergency Proclamation, whose application window closed May 1, 2026 (approved projects remain valid).
§ 02 · Exemption menu

Which exemption fits your project?

Expanded detail for the “statutory or categorical exemption” branch of the master tree. Apply the most protective / durable exemption first. Statutory exemptions (①②③) are stronger than categorical — they are not subject to the §15300.2 exceptions.

If your project involves a commercial timber or biomass sale on private or state land, start with ⑧ (Forest Practice Act) — THP/NTMP/PTEIR review is the functional CEQA equivalent and governs before any of the exemptions below apply.

① Statutory · sunset Jan 1, 2028

NEPA-reviewed federal projects SB 901 / AB 211 · PRC §4799.05(d)

The cleanest pathway for BLM / USFS partner work.

Eligible activities. Prescribed fire, reforestation, habitat restoration, thinning, fuel reduction, and related activities.

Eligible lands. Federal lands (in whole or in part).

Trigger. NEPA review complete (ROD, FONSI, CatEx). Significant impacts identified in a NEPA EIS must be avoided or mitigated.

Funding. State grants on federal land, including Good Neighbor Authority projects.

Example use: BLM / USFS projects with completed NEPA review PRC §4799.05 statute text
② Statutory · no sunset

Wildfire risk reduction SB 131 · PRC §21080.49

New in 2025. Project must fit one of four categories cleanly.
(a)
Prescribed fire / fuel reduction to restore fire return interval: ≤50 contiguous acres, within ½ mile of a subdivision of ≥30 dwelling units. Excludes coastal sage scrub & other sensitive habitat. CDFW consultation required.
(b)
Defensible space fire clearance on public roadways that are egress or evacuation routes serving communities of ≥30 residences. Clearance limited to 100 ft from the roadway. Flammable vegetation and trees <12″ DBH only. No acreage cap — constrained by the corridor width.
(c)
Home hardening / defensible space within 200 ft of a legal structure in a High or Very High Fire Hazard Severity Zone (FHSZ). Covers structural hardening and immediate defensible space activities. No tree-size limit specified. Requires FHSZ location.
(d)
Fuel break within 200 ft of structures, clearing flammable vegetation and trees <12″ DBH. Unlike (c), no FHSZ requirement — applies wherever structures are present. Use this category for fuel-break work outside a designated severity zone, or where tree-size constraints fit the project.

§15300.2 exceptions do not apply. Project must comply with all other laws, ordinances, zoning.

③ Statutory · sunset Jan 1, 2030

SERP — Habitat restoration “Cutting the Green Tape” · PRC §21080.56

No acreage cap. No location-based exceptions.

Eligible activities. Projects to conserve, restore, protect, or enhance California native fish, wildlife, and their habitat — or to restore or provide such habitat.

“Exclusively” requirement. The project as a whole must be exclusively restoration — a test of purpose and design, not of the tools used. Thinning, mastication, prescribed fire and herbicide have all been concurred where the project is designed to restore the vegetation density and fire regime native species co-evolved with, with wildfire-risk reduction to lives and property treated as the incidental public benefit under subdivision (b). Construction must be solely habitat-related.

Process. Lead agency findings (subdivisions a–d) → CDFW Director concurrence → file NOE with OPR within 48 hours. CDFW target: 60 days.

Nevada County precedent: Sierra Foothill Forest Climate Resilience (WCB / Sierra Streams Institute) — 625 ac thinning, prescribed fire, invasive control; CDFW concurrence 21080.56-2023-034-R2, Aug 2023 CDFW SERP program
④ Categorical · §15300.2 applies

Class 4 — Minor Alterations to Land CEQA Guidelines §15304

The workhorse for small fuels & forestry projects — but §15300.2 exceptions apply.

Scope. Minor alterations to land, water, and vegetation — thinning, ladder-fuel removal, roadside brushing, fuel break maintenance. Removal of healthy mature scenic trees is barred unless for forestry or agricultural purposes, in which case RPF attestation citing PRC §753 is the standard basis.

Any §15300.2 exception defeats the exemption — address each in the NOE:

Sensitive location. Mapped critical habitat, rare plant communities, riparian zones, or scenic corridors disqualify regardless of project size. Check CNDDB before filing.

Unusual circumstances. T&E species, steep-slope erosion from heavy equipment, or other site conditions creating a reasonable possibility of significant effect — document routine conditions and any CDFW consultation.

Cumulative impact. Individually minor treatments may fail collectively when one of several planned phases on the same landscape.

Scenic, historical, hazardous. Damage to state-designated scenic-highway views, listed historical resources, or Cortese-list sites also disqualifies.

Scale. No hard acreage cap, but large footprints invite challenge — document why overall impact remains minor.

Most common basis for hand-crew fuel-reduction NOEs NOE: include RPF attestation (PRC §753) and address each §15300.2 exception

March 2025 Emergency Proclamation EO N-18-25 / EO N-38-25

Historic record only — approved projects remain valid.

What it did. Suspended CEQA, CESA, Coastal Act, and other state environmental laws for fuels projects ≤3,000 ac meeting one of six wildfire-risk-reduction objectives.

Status. More than 500 projects fast-tracked (nearly 130,000 footprint acres, per the Governor’s Sept 29, 2026 release). On-the-ground work must begin by Oct 15, 2026; completion within 2 years (extensions to 5 years for CAL FIRE Wildfire Prevention, Forest Health, and some Prop 4 grants).

Compliance. Statewide Fuels Reduction Environmental Protection Plan (Nov 2025) governs BMPs for all approved projects.

Successor. SB 1370 (Stern; Ch. 777, Stats. 2026), signed Sept 29, 2026 and taking effect Jan 1, 2027, codifies a consolidated-review process for covered wildfire mitigation projects (PRC §§4240–4243). CEQA does not apply to authorized projects, the Statewide Fuels Reduction Environmental Protection Plan stays the compliance backbone, and size caps are tighter than the proclamation’s (for example, <1,000 ac for forest projects). Authority to issue expedited authorizations runs only until Jan 1, 2029. See “What changed recently” for eligibility and exclusions. AB 2410 (Ellis), an alternative codification bill, did not pass.

⑦ Special considerations & narrow-use exemptions

Oak woodland, declared emergency, planning studies Narrow-use exemptions & checks

Read these before signing off on the primary pathway.
Oak woodlands
Two distinct rules — don't confuse them.
CEQA side — PRC §21083.4. Not an exemption. Requires a public agency to determine whether a project may result in conversion of oak woodlands and apply specified mitigation if significant. Forestry/fuels projects that maintain the woodland (thinning encroaching conifers, removing ladder fuels) are generally not “conversion” and proceed under Class 4. Check the county Oak Resources Management Plan for “Fire Safe Activities” exemptions.
Forest Practice side — 14 CCR §1038(e). A true exemption from THP preparation requirements for commercial timber operations restoring oak woodland. See Card ⑧. Operates under the FPA's Certified Regulatory Program for CEQA purposes.
§15269(a)
Declared emergency. Work to prevent or mitigate damage from a sudden, unexpected occurrence. The 2015 statewide tree mortality proclamation remains active for high-hazard zones.
§15262
Feasibility / planning study. Useful for CWPP-style documents that don’t commit the agency to physical action.
§15061(b)(3)
“Common-sense” exemption. Available where it can be seen with certainty that a project has no possibility of significant effect.
PRC §21083.4
⑧ Forest Practice · CAL FIRE

Forest Practice Act exemptions 14 CCR §1038

Functional CEQA equivalent for commercial timber projects.
§1038(c)
Structure Protection. Trees within 150 ft of an approved & legally permitted structure. No clearcut / seed tree / shelterwood removal. Subsection (c)(6) reaches 150–300 ft from a habitable structure where the post-harvest stand meets stocking standards, QMD increases, and an RPF certifies more moderate fire behavior.
§1038(b) / (d)
Dead, Dying, or Diseased / Drought Mortality. Up to 10% of volume per acre, or only trees affected by drought mortality (RPF stump-mark certification required).
§1038(e)
Oak Woodland Exemption. Cutting/removal of trees to restore and conserve California black oak (Quercus kelloggii) or Oregon white oak (Quercus garryana) woodlands and associated grasslands. ≤300 ac per ownership per planning watershed per 5-year period. Stand must have ≥35 ft²/ac of living oak basal area; post-harvest conifer stocking <25% of total; harvested conifers within 300 ft of a qualifying oak ≥4″ DBH. AB 2276 (Wood, 2024) revised the exemption; the current rule text (operative Jan 15, 2025 by emergency rulemaking) allows removal of conifers up to 30″ DBH — larger only where necessary for restoration, with written RPF justification and consultation with the Director — and requires retaining ≥80% of pre-treatment oak basal area.
§1038(g)
Wildfire-killed trees. Dead or dying trees within 300 ft of an approved structure (or a structure the fire damaged), under a current emergency proclamation or within three years of the fire with RPF certification.
§1038.3
Forest Resilience Exemption (formerly Forest Fire Prevention). Harvest area up to 500 ac of timberland rated Moderate / High / Very High on the FHSZ map; only trees under 30″ DBH (dead or dying to 36″, ≤10% of volume); retain the six largest trees per acre and ≥30% canopy on 80% of the area; temporary roads only; RPF prepares the notice and marks the trees. Renamed by AB 2276 and extended to Jan 1, 2031.

For non-exempt commercial work: THP (5-yr), NTMP (≤2,500 ac), or PTEIR.

The FPA program is a Certified Regulatory Program under PRC §21080.5 — THP/NTMP review is the functional equivalent of an EIR.

Board of Forestry regs An RPF (Registered Professional Forester) is required to prepare or oversee FPA documents and stump-mark exempt harvests — consult one early.
§ 03 · Working notes for Nevada County practitioners

Applying the pathways on the ground.

Practitioner notes from the Permitting & Environmental Compliance Subcommittee — how the pathways map to the kinds of projects QWSG participants are running across the county.

Federal land partnerships

For projects on BLM / USFS land with completed NEPA: lead with §4799.05(d) (SB 901 / AB 211).

Cleanest pathway through its Jan 1, 2028 sunset, including Good Neighbor Authority projects. Document the NEPA basis carefully and confirm any EIS-identified impacts are addressed in project design. The 2025 bill to remove the sunset (AB 404, Sanchez) failed, so plan around the 2028 expiration. The June 2026 draft Action Plan (3.9) anticipates federal partners expanding categorical exclusions and programmatic NEPA, which would widen this pathway's reach.

Restoration-primary

SERP is now the strongest tool for exclusively-restoration work.

No acreage cap, no §15300.2 exceptions. Requires CDFW Director concurrence (60-day target). The Sierra Foothill Forest Climate Resilience Project (Wildlife Conservation Board lead, Sierra Streams Institute, Jones Bar Firewise community) carried 625 ac of thinning, mastication, prescribed fire and Scotch broom control through SERP in 2023 by designing it as foothill-forest habitat restoration, with wildfire-risk reduction as the incidental public benefit. The design did the work — heterogeneous clumps, retained snags and corridors, a topographic-wetness thinning plan, named beneficiary species, BMPs, monitoring and landowner maintenance agreements — so pre-consult CDFW before assuming a fuels grant can be reframed. For projects ≤5 acres, Class 33 is the lighter-weight categorical alternative.

Private / WUI

SB 131 §21080.49 is powerful for the urban interface, but geographically limited.

Particularly (c) home hardening / defensible space and (d) 200-ft fuel breaks — strong fits for projects adjacent to Grass Valley, Nevada City, Penn Valley, and other county subdivisions. The ≤50-ac + ½-mile-from-30-DU constraint on subdivision (a) prescribed fire will limit applicability for more remote sites in the county.

Oak woodland

Conifer encroachment removal generally proceeds under Class 4.

Ladder fuel reduction that maintains the woodland is generally not “conversion” under PRC §21083.4. Review Nevada County’s local oak ordinance treatment before filing — many counties expressly exempt fire-safe activities from local mitigation requirements.

Mid-sized landscape · 50–3,000 ac

If you can’t fit SB 131 or §4799.05(d): CalVTP PEIR.

Still the best fit for landscape projects in that band. Watch for the updated PEIR — a Subsequent Program EIR whose scoping closed Feb 26, 2026 — intended to expand the treatable landscape and streamline PSA preparation. Until the writ is discharged, chaparral and coastal sage scrub treatments may rely on the 2019 PEIR only within the narrow categories the court allowed (limited-width strategic fuel breaks, maintenance of existing treatments, WUI treatments, non-native removal for ecological restoration, and certain post-fire stands); apply Update SPR CHAP-CSS-1 in those cases.

Coming Jan 2027 · SB 1370

Plan-identified fuels projects: line up SB 1370 consolidated review.

From Jan 1, 2027 until the authority lapses Jan 1, 2029, projects identified in a community wildfire preparedness plan or a plan adopted by a local agency can seek a single CNRA/CalEPA expedited authorization, with no CEQA document. Whether the Nevada County CWPP’s priority-area approach counts as “identifying” a specific project is an open question; confirm it with CAL FIRE / CNRA once the application process is posted. Plan for the 20-day public comment window, the <1,000-ac cap on forest projects, the pesticide exclusion, local ordinance consistency, and continued Forest Practice Act compliance for any timber operations. The window is only two years, so projects that are shovel-ready will benefit most.

Commercial timber component

If the project includes a commercial sale: Forest Practice is the foundation.

THP, NTMP, PTEIR, or a 14 CCR §1038 exemption sits beneath the project. A CEQA exemption (e.g. SB 131 wildfire category) may layer on top depending on funding source and land status. For private-land oak woodland restoration projects in the watershed — removing encroaching conifers to recover the woodland — the §1038(e) Oak Woodland Exemption is purpose-built for that work, especially after the AB 2276 expansion. Engage an RPF early — a Registered Professional Forester is required to prepare or co-sign FPA documents and must stump-mark harvests under most §1038 exemptions. The June 2026 draft Action Plan (Key Action 3.8) directs the Board of Forestry to convene a workgroup to streamline THP review timelines and reduce redundancies with regional water board and CDFW review. AB 442 (Ch. 504, Stats. 2026), signed Sept 27, 2026, raises NTMP caps to 4,000 ac and WFMP caps to 15,000 ac and drops the single-hydrologic-area limit on WFMP harvest areas, effective Jan 1, 2027 — relevant if a working-forest plan is your foundation.

Process · always

Document the exemption basis with a memo to file.

For statutory exemptions, file a Notice of Exemption with OPR or the county clerk to start the 35-day statute of limitations.
